The program

Four product groups, in the order a deal moves through them

Each one is a block of the semester. You build the model, present the recommendation, and take questions from a panel that has already read your work.

01

Mergers & Acquisitions

Merger model

A company is acquired. You value the target, set the price, choose the financing mix, and show what the deal does to earnings.

02

Leveraged Finance

Leveraged buyout

A sponsor finances the buyout. You build the capital structure, run the debt schedule, and account for where the return actually comes from.

03

Debt Capital Markets

High yield issuance

The company raises debt. You assess the credit, place it against comparable bonds, and argue for where the issue should price.

04

Equity Capital Markets

Convertible issuance

Capital comes back through the equity market. You structure an equity-linked offering and weigh it against the straight alternatives.

Industrials and energy

Two sectors built on capital

Manufacturing, aerospace and defense, transportation and logistics, and building products on one side. Upstream, midstream, oilfield services, power, and renewables on the other.

Both are capital intensive and heavily financed, which makes them unusually well suited to product work. Staying inside them means a member finishes the year knowing something specific rather than a little about everything.

What the structure is for

Technical work, not commentary

Product groups own how a transaction is structured and priced. That is where the analytical work sits, and it is what gets tested when you interview.

Small teams, real accountability

You work in a team of two and present alongside your partner. Both of you answer questions, and there is nowhere to hide in a pair.

Feedback that is written down

Every pitch gets written critique within a day. The record is what makes the second pitch better than the first.

About

A club built around the work, not around the roster.

Carolina Industrials & Energy Group is a student run organization at UNC Chapel Hill focused on teaching members the technical work that sits inside an investment bank.

Product over coverage

Why product groups

Nearly every finance organization on campus splits into sector teams. We split into the four product groups a bank is actually built from: Mergers & Acquisitions, Leveraged Finance, Debt Capital Markets, and Equity Capital Markets.

The difference is what each side owns. A coverage team owns the client relationship and the sector story. A product team owns the structuring: how a transaction is financed, what the debt costs, how an offering prices, and what the returns look like. That is the technical half of banking, and it is the half a student can actually practice.

The sector mandate

Why industrials and energy

Every company we work on comes from one of those two sectors. The constraint is deliberate. Both are capital intensive and heavily financed, which makes them well suited to product work, and staying narrow means members build on what they already know instead of starting over each block.

IndustrialsManufacturing, aerospace and defense, transportation and logistics, building products, industrial services
EnergyUpstream, midstream, oilfield services, refining, power, utilities, renewables

Scope

What we are not

Not a fund
We hold no capital and manage no portfolio. Everything we produce is a simulation.
Not a lecture series
Instruction is delivered in service of work that is due. Nothing is taught in the abstract.
Not a sector coverage group
Sector work exists to supply companies for product analysis, not as an end in itself.

Program

Four blocks. One model and one pitch in each.

The semester is divided into four blocks, each covering one product group. Everyone moves through them together, so one discipline is taught at a time and applied immediately.

Inside a block

How a block runs

Pick a company
Each team proposes a public company in industrials or energy and has it approved before work begins.
Learn the product
The block opens with a session on the transaction type and the model behind it.
Build
Two to three weeks. One partner owns the model, the other owns the deck, and the roles swap next block.
Pitch
Fifteen minutes presenting, fifteen minutes of questions from a panel that has already reviewed your model.
Critique
Written feedback within a day, covering the analysis, the delivery, and how the work held up under questioning.

The four blocks

What you build

01

Mergers & Acquisitions

Merger model

Valuation, purchase price and premium, sources and uses, and the effect of the transaction on earnings per share.

02

Leveraged Finance

Leveraged buyout

Capital structure across the debt tranches, a full debt schedule, credit statistics through the hold, and sponsor returns.

03

Debt Capital Markets

High yield issuance

Credit profile and ratings view, relative value against comparable bonds, proposed pricing, and the call and covenant structure.

04

Equity Capital Markets

Convertible issuance

Offering terms, dilution, and the effective cost of capital measured against straight debt and straight equity.

The question you should expect

What is this answer most sensitive to, and what happens if that assumption is wrong? It comes up in every block. If you can answer it, you built the model. If you cannot, you filled in a template.

After the rotation

Beyond the first semester

Once a member has been through all four blocks, they place into a standing product group and take the second transaction type in each: a separation analysis in M&A, a dividend recapitalization in Leveraged Finance, a liability management exercise in Debt Capital Markets, and an initial public offering in Equity Capital Markets.

Nobody repeats a pitch they have already given.

Process

What the process looks like

Written application
A short form covering your background, your interest in the sectors, and why the product structure appeals to you.
Interview
A conversation with two members of the club, plus one short analytical exercise written for someone with no finance background.
Placement
Accepted members are paired into a team and begin the first block.

Commitment

What we expect

Time
One meeting a week, plus roughly eight to twelve hours per block, most of it in the week before the pitch.
Attendance
Come to every pitch in your block, not only your own. Watching other teams get questioned on the same product is half the value.
Recruiting
Always an excused conflict. Tell us in advance.
